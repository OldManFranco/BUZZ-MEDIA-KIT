# Medellin Buzz Shorts — Editing Kit (v0.5)

**Purpose:** the procedure for cutting vertical shorts (YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, TikTok) for the **Medellin Buzz** channel from Andrew's footage. When Franco asks for a new short, follow this doc.

**Status:** v0.5 (2026-10-27). Three shorts made end to end from El Hueco:
- **s1:** approved after one round of notes, about B-roll.
- **s2:** started cold from this doc and approved after one note, about the ending.
- **s3:** re-planned from an old draft against these rules, first short with HyperFrames graphics (title card + a custom map). **v1 approved with no notes.**

Both rounds of notes are now rules: §5 "Franco's B-roll rules" and "Endings close the thought", plus the v1 self-review in Step 6. **Goal: Franco approves v1, or needs at most one round of notes.** Rules still marked `[DRAFT]` haven't been tested on a render yet.

**Forked from:** The Standard kit (motivational TikToks). Everything specific to it (ElevenLabs, Declan Sage narration, athlete research, Anton captions, the watermark, era looks) is in `_archive_the_standard\`, untouched, for diffing. Nothing from there applies unless this doc brings it back.

**Language:** Franco works in English. Chat, reports and generated files in English. All on-screen text in English.

## 1. Hard rules

1. **Andrew's voice only.** Every word heard in a short is Andrew, recorded on camera or in his vlog footage. No ElevenLabs, no TTS, no AI narration, no voice cloning, no "pickup" lines generated by anything. If a short needs a line Andrew never said, it doesn't get made; propose a title card or a different clip instead.
2. **No synthetic Andrew.** No lip-sync fixes, no face edits, no AI-generated shots of him. (Alejo's subtle iris-only eye-contact correction is long-form only and is not part of this kit.)
3. **Output:** 1080×1920, 9:16, 30 fps, H.264 yuv420p, AAC 48 kHz stereo, MP4 ready to upload. Target **30–60 s**, floor 20 s. 60 s is the default ceiling (Franco's spec; 60 s keeps one file valid on all three platforms). **A complete ending beats the ceiling:** Franco OK'd going past 60 s when the ending needs it (2026-10-27, s2). Say so in the plan when a short goes over.
4. **Hook rule (hard):** the first 3 seconds must capture attention. One of:
   - a punchy line from Andrew that states the payoff, a number, money, a place or a conflict (the line must make sense with zero context);
   - a loud visual (strong motion, a reveal, a striking place) with Andrew's hook line landing by 1.5 s;
   - a branded title card hook (§7) over the first shot, while Andrew's line starts.
   **Never:** "hey what's up", "so", "today I want to talk about", a mid-story opener that needs the long-form for context ("At first, the crimes were simple…"), a question with no stakes, or a slow walk-in.
   If a candidate clip has no 3-second hook, **propose how to construct one from the raw footage before cutting** (see §5 step 4): pull the strongest later line forward, splice a cold-open, or add a title card. Never invent words.
5. **Content exclusions (hard, from Medellin Buzz production standards).** A short is never about, and a hook never leans on:
   - real estate investment analysis (ROI, yields, rental returns, "what $X buys", investor visa math)
   - visa or legal warnings (immigration rules, tax residency, legal risk)
   - scam content (dating-app scams, scopolamine, robberies of foreigners)
   - dating or relationship content (including sex tourism / "working women" angles)
   - livestream chat reads
   - merch or charity plugs
   - deportation backstory (Andrew's or anyone's)
   If the source material is about one of these, skip it. If a single sentence in an otherwise good clip touches one, cut around it; if you can't, skip the clip. Flag every skip in the plan with the reason.
6. **No made-up facts on screen.** Numbers, names and dates in title cards or chips come from what Andrew says in the clip or from `script.md`. If a number on screen isn't in either, verify it on the web (2 sources) and note the source in the plan, or leave it off.
7. **Brand:** fonts and colors from `brand\` and `_brand\brand-notes.md` (palette and caption style approved by Franco 2026-10-27). Never green highlights (Opus Clip's look), never Impact, never a watermark.
8. **Approval gate:** nothing is rendered before Franco approves the EDL plan (§5, Gate 1). Ask before running ffmpeg to write any new video file outside an approved render.

Opus Clip only ever sees the long-form. This is our main advantage — use it.

## 5. Pipeline

Order: **inventory → transcribe (WhisperX) → scene-detect → map long-form to raw → hook candidates (3–5) → EDL plans → GATE 1 (Franco approves) → render → QA → GATE 2 (Franco reviews) → package.**

### Step 1: Inventory
- List every file, `ffprobe` each (duration, resolution, fps, orientation, audio channels). Flag: vertical vs horizontal raw, anything under 1080p, variable frame rate (phones), missing audio.
- Write `working\<slug>\inventory.json`. Report what's missing in the first reply.

### Step 2: Transcribe + scene-detect
- **Transcription = WhisperX** (Whisper large-v3 + wav2vec2 forced alignment), using Alejo's setup read-only:
  ```
  "E:\FRANCO\MEDELLIN_BUZZ CLAUDE KIT\CLAUDE\tools\venv_whisperx\Scripts\python.exe" -I ^
    "E:\FRANCO\MEDELLIN_BUZZ CLAUDE KIT\CLAUDE\tools\transcribir_whisperx.py" <clip or folder> working\<slug>\transcripts
  ```
  Runs on the RTX 3060 (CUDA). Outputs `<clip>.words.json` (word-level `w/s/e/p`) and `<clip>.txt`. Resumable. Numbers like "$3,000" can't be aligned and are interpolated (`"interpolada": true`) — never cut on an interpolated word edge; snap to the nearest aligned word or a silence.
  The torchcodec warning at startup is harmless (seen 2026-10-27).
  Transcribe `longform.mp4` and every clip in `raw\`. Skip `broll\` (the script already skips names containing "broll").
- **Scene detection:** `pipeline\scene_detect.py` (PySceneDetect ContentDetector, threshold 27) on `longform.mp4` and on each `broll\` clip → `shots.json`.

### Step 3: Map long-form → raw
- For each long-form segment, find the same words in the raw transcripts (fuzzy text match on word sequences). Record `{longform_t, raw_file, raw_t, match_score}` in `working\<slug>\map.json`.
- This tells us, for every good moment: the uncut take, what Andrew said just before/after (often a better hook), and which raw clips around it are B-roll-worthy (walking shots, the place itself).

### Step 4: Hook candidates (3–5)
Pick **3–5 candidate shorts** per long-form. Each must be:
- **Self-contained:** makes sense with zero context. Percent-watched separates winners (Alejo's data, bible §18).
- **One idea:** one claim, one story, one comparison. Not a summary of the long-form.
- **Outside the exclusions** (§1.5).
- **Strong-first-line:** money, a number, a law-free fact, a place, or a US comparison in the first line (data from Alejo's channel analysis: "comparison with something in the US wins; the owner's story loses").

For each candidate, write the hook explicitly:
- `hook_type`: `line` (Andrew's own line, as-is) · `pulled_forward` (a stronger line from later in the clip/raw, moved to 0:00 as a cold-open, then the clip plays from its natural start) · `title_card` (branded card over the first shot while Andrew starts) · `visual` (a loud shot first, Andrew's line by 1.5 s).
- The exact words heard in 0:00–0:03 and what's on screen.
- If the natural start fails the hook rule, say so and show the construction.

### Step 5: EDL plans → GATE 1
For each candidate, write `working\<slug>\plans\s<N>_edl.json` and a readable summary. Structure: **hook (0–3 s) → body → optional CTA / ending.**

```json
{
  "slug": "el-hueco-wholesale", "short": "s1", "status": "draft",
  "title_options": ["...", "...", "..."],
  "target_s": 42,
  "hook": {"type": "pulled_forward", "spoken": "This shirt is $22 here. 15 minutes away it's four times that.",
           "title_card": null, "why": "number + US-style price comparison in first line"},
  "events": [
    {"t": 0.00, "kind": "aroll", "src": "raw/C0042.MP4", "in": 12.40, "out": 16.85, "reframe": {"xc": 0.52}, "note": "cold-open line"},
    {"t": 4.45, "kind": "broll", "src": "broll/hueco_walk.mp4", "in": 3.0, "out": 5.2, "audio": "nat -18 dB", "under_voice_of": "raw/C0042.MP4 31.2-33.4"},
    {"t": 6.65, "kind": "aroll", "src": "longform.mp4", "in": 412.1, "out": 425.0, "reframe": {"xc": 0.48}},
    {"t": 19.5, "kind": "chip", "text": "4× CHEAPER", "sfx": "pop"},
    {"t": 38.0, "kind": "ending", "type": "loop", "note": "last line lands, cut on the beat, loops to first frame"}
  ],
  "captions": "auto from words.json, brand spec",
  "music": {"file": "...", "level_db": -24, "license": "..."},
  "exclusion_check": "clean — skipped 0:31-0:36 (visa remark)",
  "facts_on_screen": [{"text": "$22", "source": "Andrew in clip, 70,000 COP at 3,180"}]
}
```

Rules for the body:
- **Cut on ideas, not words.** Never cut mid-sentence or mid-thought. Never end on a dangling setup ("And on top of that…"). Remove "uh/um", false starts, repeated words. Natural gaps inside a thought 0.15–0.35 s (tighter than long-form).
- **Pacing `[DRAFT]`:** a visual change (cut, B-roll, punch-in, chip, card) every 2–4 s. Never more than 5 s of the same framing. Punch-ins 108–115% on strong lines only.
- **B-roll from the raw folder:** when Andrew names a place, a price, an object — show it. Prefer his own raw footage of the place over anything else.
- **Franco's B-roll rules** (from el-hueco s1 Gate 2, 2026-10-27; these were his notes on v1, so plan them in from the start):
  1. **Andrew names the place → cut to the place.** "This place is called El Hueco" plays over B-roll of El Hueco, not over Andrew on camera.
  2. **Andrew names a company or thing we have no footage of → quick stills.** "Temu and Amazon" got a ~0.8 s Temu card and a ~1.6 s Amazon fulfillment-center photo, each cut on its word. Source: Wikimedia Commons (search through its API), **CC0, public domain or CC BY only** (no share-alike). Log each file's license in `working\<slug>\stills\commons\sources.json`, and put CC BY credits in the package. Never show a stand-in as the real thing: Commons had no Temu warehouse, so it was the Temu logo on an ink card, not some other warehouse.
  3. **B-roll starts on the first word of the sentence it belongs to** ("But you can't feel it" starts on "But"), not halfway in on the matching word. It can end where it already ends.
  4. **The clip must match the words literally.** "And now their kids…" → a father walking with his little boy, not generic shoppers. Before choosing, make 9:16-crop contact sheets of every B-roll clip (a frame every 1.5–2 s) and pick from those, not from a single frame. Then check each pick as real crops with `pipeline\broll_strips.py` (one row per candidate window at its `xc`): it shows exactly what the short will show (s3 picked all 12 B-roll shots this way).
  5. **The closing stretch can stay on B-roll to the end.** Andrew doesn't need to come back for the payoff line. Pick the last clip's in-point so the final frame lands on something strong (s1 ends on a vendor in a Nacional jersey smiling at camera).
  6. **No chip that repeats a word Andrew is saying** (the "×10" chip was cut). A chip has to add something the caption doesn't. A counter works for lists: s2 used "TIP 1 OF 4" … "TIP 4 OF 4" instead of chips that restated each phrase.
  7. **No still that's only roughly right.** If Commons has nothing that matches the words (s2: "the exact amount of cash in your pocket" vs. piles of cash bricks), stay on Andrew with a punch-in instead.
  8. **Reusing a clip from another short of the same video is OK** (s1 and s2 share the jersey-vendor clip, at different moments). Flag it in the package so those shorts go out on different days.
- **Endings close the thought** (Franco's note on el-hueco s2 v1, 2026-10-27). s2 v1 ended on "A lot of the times it works." and felt left hanging. Andrew keeps going in the raw: "Sometimes they call your bluff, but that's part of the whole negotiation process. And for me, that's part of the fun." v2 ended there, on his smile, and was approved.
  - Before fixing the ending, read 20–30 s of the raw transcript past the planned out-point.
  - If the next lines complete the idea (a twist, a personal punchline, "for me…"), include them, even if that means going past 60 s (§1.3).
  - A line that has a "but…" coming isn't an ending. When Andrew's last line is personal, end on his face (punch-in, hold on the smile). When it's about the place, end on B-roll (rule 5).
- **Ending:** either a clean payoff line that lands with a 0.4–0.8 s hold, or a loop (last line flows into the first frame). No Medellin Buzz banner or branded end card unless Andrew asks for one. CTA only if Andrew actually says one; comment-prompt style ("tell me in the comments…") only when it's his line. No merch/charity CTAs, no "link in bio" invented on screen.
- **Length:** whatever the idea needs inside 30–60 s. Shorter is better if it's complete.

**HyperFrames graphics pass (every short, Franco 2026-10-27).** HyperFrames is part of every Medellin Buzz short, not a per-short option. While writing each EDL, go through the script line by line and decide where a graphic, card, animation or motion moment would make the short clearer or more gripping, and design it:
- **Use what fits the line**, from simplest to most custom: the library (title card, chip, data card with rolling counter), a block or component from the HyperFrames registry (maps, charts, kinetic type, transitions, camera moves: search before hand-building), or a one-off scene written for this short (`"kind": "hf_scene"`, see `pipeline\graphics\README.md`).
- **Good triggers:** a number, a price or a comparison (counter, bars, before/after), a place or a route (map), a process or a list (steps, counter), a key phrase worth landing (kinetic type), a hook that needs help in silent autoplay (title card).
- **Restraint is part of it.** Every graphic earns its place. Andrew on camera and real B-roll still come first (B-roll rules 1–8); a graphic never covers his face or repeats what the caption already says (rule 6), one orange element at a time, brand look only. "No graphic here" is a valid decision; say why.
- **Gate 1 shows the plan:** for each candidate, list every graphic (time, what it shows, which engine piece) and, for anything new, a still mockup (`build_graphics.py --stage snapshot`). Franco approves the graphics with the cut.
- A new graphic that works well gets added to `pipeline\graphics\` so the next short can reuse it.

**→ GATE 1: Franco sees** 3–5 candidates as a table (hook words, hook type, length, why it works, risks, exclusion notes, **planned graphics**) + one EDL per candidate. Franco picks which to render and edits. Nothing renders before this.

### Step 6: Render (after Gate 1)
- **Reframe 16:9 → 9:16:** crop around Andrew's face (Alejo's `CLAUDE\tools\render\cara.py` / MediaPipe face landmarker is available read-only; otherwise contact sheets via `pipeline\build_contact_sheet.py`). Keep eyes at ~35–40% from the top. Vertical raw footage is used as-is. No blurred-background letterboxing.
- **Grade:** match the long-form's look with `pipeline\match_grade.py`: per-channel histogram match of aligned raw ↔ long-form frames (from `map.json`) → `working\<slug>\grade\longform_match.cube`. Alejo's LUT candidate `mb_look_video1_candidato.cube` was tested on el-hueco s1 (2026-10-27) and rejected: it crushed the greens and posterized shadows on this DJI footage.
- **Captions:** karaoke word-by-word, brand spec in `_brand\brand-notes.md` §4 (Poppins ExtraBold, white + ink outline, active word on an orange `#D95633` plate, 2–4 words). **Baseline y 1360** (top of the lower third, below Andrew's chin on punch-ins; Franco, 2026-10-27). Never lower than y 1440: Opus puts captions at 78–85% of the height, which is where the apps' username/description layer covers them. Timed from the WhisperX words of the **final** audio, never from the script.
- **Caption text traps** (caught in s2; fix them in the EDL, never by hand):
  - WhisperX writes peso amounts as dollars ("$190" when he says "190" pesos). Never show `$` for pesos. Use `"caption_fixes": {"$190,": "190,"}`.
  - Spanish phrases lose their ¿? and split badly. Fix the spelling with `caption_fixes`, and keep a phrase in one group with `"caption_join": ["¿Y SI LE COMPRO DOS?"]`.
  - WhisperX can drop a word Andrew clearly says (s2: "a lot of **the** times"). Fix that one word with a time-pinned fix: `"caption_fixes": {"of@51.48": "of the"}` changes only the word that starts within 0.15 s of 51.48. If QA's re-transcription hears an extra word where there's no splice, the first transcription missed it, so add the fix.
  - Payoff phrases stay together with `"caption_break_before": ["ONE HANDSHAKE"]`.
  - Title-card line breaks are forced with `|`: `"SAY THIS. | PAY LESS."`.
  - WhisperX writes spoken numbers as digits ("a hundred" → `100`). If Andrew says the words, show the words: `"caption_fixes": {"100": "a hundred"}` (s3). Digits are for prices and figures (`$22`, `8,000`), not figures of speech.
  - Spanish phrases stay in one group even without ¿?: `"caption_join": ["COMPRAR AL MAYOR"]` (s3 split it as "COMPRAR AL / MAYOR"). Also join short place endings ("AROUND HERE") so no single word is left alone.
- **Motion graphics = HyperFrames** (since v0.4, Franco 2026-10-27): title card (§7), chips, and data cards in the long-form card system re-laid for 9:16. One orange element on screen at a time. Motion: ease-out, 0.3–0.5 s, no bounce/elastic/glitch.
  - Library: `pipeline\graphics\` (brand tokens, templates, the registry's `number-wheel` rolling counter, local GSAP). How to use and extend it: `pipeline\graphics\README.md`.
  - `render_short.py` stage `graphics` → `build_graphics.py`: writes a HyperFrames project in `working\<slug>\render\s<N>\hf\`, runs `hyperframes check` (stops on errors), renders a transparent `graphics.mov`. The final stage stacks picture → HyperFrames graphics → Pillow captions.
  - Every short uses it: `plan_shorts.py` writes `"graphics_engine": "hyperframes"` on every new plan. Only the already-delivered s1 and s2 lack it, so they re-render exactly as approved.
  - Custom scenes: `{"kind": "hf_scene", "src": "working/<slug>/graphics/<name>.html", "t": …, "t_out": …}`, optional `"full_bleed": true` (mutes captions), `"has_orange": true` (keeps the caption plate ink), `"assets": [...]`. The file is a normal HyperFrames scene with `{{id}}` as its id.
  - Data cards are full-bleed (they replace the picture, captions mute under them): use one for a hero number Andrew says, `"counter"` makes it roll. Facts on cards follow §1.6 and carry a `source_line`.
  - Before a render, `python pipeline/build_graphics.py <edl> --stage snapshot` gives PNG frames of every graphic (no video written). Look at them in the self-review.
  - Search the HyperFrames registry before hand-building a new kind of graphic (`pipeline\graphics\README.md`, "Adding a block"). The CLI runs through the Claude plugin launcher; `build_graphics.hf_cli()` finds it.
- **Sound design:** see §8.
- Render parts to `working\<slug>\render\`, final to `output\<slug>\`.
- **Self-review before sending v1** (Franco's eye, not just QA): grab a frame at every picture change and look at them all.
  - Every place, company or object Andrew names has its picture (Franco's B-roll rules 1–2).
  - Every B-roll starts on its sentence's first word (rule 3) and matches the words literally (rule 4).
  - No chip repeats a spoken word (rule 6).
  - Captions sit at y 1360 and never cover his mouth.
  - Stills aren't cropped badly (a logo or sign cut at the edge).
  - The last frame is a strong one (rule 5).
  - The ending closes the thought: read what Andrew says next in the raw ("Endings close the thought").
  - Caption text matches what he says: no `$` on pesos, Spanish with ¿?, no dropped words ("Caption text traps").
  - The title card breaks lines where the phrase breaks.
  - Fix what fails, then run QA.
- The ending hold is picture only: `render_short.py` stops Andrew's voice at the last word, with a 60 ms fade. Before that fix, s2's hold carried the start of his next word ("Sometimes…").

### Step 7: QA (automatic, `pipeline\qa.py`)
- 1080×1920, 30 fps, ≤60 s, audio present, −14 ±1 LUFS, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP.
- First word or title card on screen by 1.0 s; hook words in 0–3 s match the approved EDL.
- Re-transcribe the final MP4 with WhisperX: transcript only contains approved lines; no exclusion keywords (visa, deport, scam, Tinder/dating, ROI/return/yield, merch, donate…); no cut mid-word (alignment gap check at every splice).
- Captions: inside the safe zone (y 260–1440, x ≤ 940), never more than 4 words, no overlap, active-word timing within ±0.08 s. Re-transcribing has its own noise, so this passes with a median ≤ 30 ms and ≤ 3% of words over 80 ms (s2 failed on 5 of 218 words with a 5 ms median, which was noise).
- No frame of black/flash at splices; no shot under 0.4 s except intentional.
- 8 frame grabs (0.0, 0.5, 1.5, 3.0 s, then evenly spaced) in `_qa.md` for Franco.

### Step 8: GATE 2 + package
Franco watches the MP4, often on his phone over Remote Control, which can't send files over 30 MB. Send the master *and* a phone copy (`working\<slug>\render\s<N>\review\<slug>-s<N>_v<k>_phone.mp4`: H.264 4 Mb/s, AAC 160k, `+faststart`, ≈20 MB for 40 s); the master in `output\` is what gets uploaded. Then write `_package.md`:
- 3 title options (≤50 chars, specific, honest; brand voice: no "Here's the thing", no "Honestly", no dramatic dashes, no three-part parallel lists).
- Description per platform (YouTube Shorts / IG Reels / TikTok), 1–2 lines + 3–5 hashtags. Never #fyp or #viral.
- Pinned comment (a real question Andrew asked, if any).
- Cover frame suggestion (time + why).
- Posting note: spread shorts out; 3+ on the same day costs ~17% (Alejo's data).

## 6. Hook construction toolbox

In order of preference:
1. **Andrew's natural first line** if it already passes §1.4.
2. **Pull forward:** the clip's strongest line (usually the number, the comparison or the payoff) plays first as a 2–4 s cold-open, hard cut, then the clip starts at its natural beginning. The pulled line must not be repeated later in full.
3. **Better take from raw:** the same idea said punchier in an unused raw take.
4. **Visual-first:** the most striking raw shot of the place (motion, scale, a reveal) for ≤1.5 s, Andrew's line already under it.
5. **Title card:** see §7. Use when the spoken hook is good but needs a frame (a number, a place) to land in silent autoplay.

Silent-autoplay check: with the sound off, do the first 3 seconds still make someone stop? (captions + title card + visual). If not, fix it.

## 7. Branded title card (second 0–1) `[DRAFT]`

- Not a separate intro card that delays Andrew. It's a **text layer over the first shot**, on screen 0.0–2.5 s, while Andrew already talks.
- Design: ink `#1D1D1D` plate (or cream `#F4EFE2` on dark footage), Poppins ExtraBold 72–96 px, 2–5 words, UPPERCASE, the key number/word in orange `#D95633`. Optional warm-gray tracked eyebrow above (Poppins Medium, e.g. `EL HUECO · MEDELLÍN`).
- Position y ≈ 420–760 (upper-middle, clear of the captions and of Andrew's face).
- In: masked reveal, 0.3 s ease-out, with a soft hit SFX. Out: 0.2 s, or hard cut on the next shot.
- It must say something the spoken line doesn't (triple-hook alignment: spoken hook, on-screen text and visual all point at the same single idea, but don't repeat the same words).

## 8. Audio

- **Voice first.** Andrew's dialogue: clean-up (high-pass 80 Hz, gentle de-noise on outdoor vlog audio, light compression 2:1), normalized consistent across splices (no level jumps between long-form and raw sources — match them).
- **Room tone / natural sound** under B-roll from the raw footage, −18 to −24 dB, to avoid dead air at cuts.
