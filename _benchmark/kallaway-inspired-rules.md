# Kallaway-inspired craft rules (Buzz Media)

What we adopt from `kallaway-analysis.md`. **Inspired, not copied:** his pacing and structure, our brand, our layouts, our client's voice. Every rule here is a default; a client's `_instructions\` or `_brand\` overrides it. Rules marked `[EAR]` rest on things the audio analysis couldn't confirm; Franco checks them on the first renders.

## A. The hook (0–3 s)

1. **First word by 0.15 s.** Trim every pre-roll breath, "uh", look-down. The reel starts mid-breath of the first word.
2. **About 8–10 words in the first 3 s.** If the client's first line is slow, use a faster take or pull a tighter line forward.
3. **Two-stage hook.** Line 1 = the claim (a thing + a superlative/number, or a name + "just did"). Within 5 s, line 2 = the twist that raises stakes ("And the crazy part is…", "Guess who gets the clients"). If the client never says a twist line, the twist goes on screen as the hook headline, never invented as voice.
4. **Hook headline from frame 0, 2 lines, 4–7 words.** Line 1 in white, line 2 (the payoff) in the client's accent. On an ink plate (never red for Buzz Media: red is logo-only). Sits on the split-screen seam or at y ≈ 300–600 in full-frame. It **labels the category or the stake; it doesn't repeat the spoken words.**
5. **Proof on screen inside 2 s** when the hook makes a claim: the stat screenshot with the number highlighted (accent marker behind the number), the before/after, the real product.
6. **First picture change by 1.2 s.**

## B. Cut cadence

7. **A picture change every 1.5–2 s on average; every 1.2–1.5 s in the first 10 s.** A change = a cut, a layout switch, a B-roll swap, a punch-in, or a card. (Kallaway: median 1.7 s, 1.4 s in the opening.)
8. **No shot holds over 4 s** unless it's a screen demo the viewer needs to read (then 6 s max, with a zoom or a cursor move inside it).
9. **Cut on speech structure:** picture changes go on sentence or clause starts first, then on emphasis words. Never inside a word on the face layer. B-roll may swap mid-sentence (the voice carries it).
10. **Cut the voice tight:** gaps between words 40–120 ms inside a sentence, 150–250 ms between sentences; remove breaths. Use jump cuts on the face layer, each hidden by a layout switch, a B-roll swap or a 5–8 % punch-in. (Kallaway runs 3.5 words/s; our clients won't all talk that fast. Tighten gaps, never speed up the voice.)
11. **One "breather" beat per reel:** a 2–3-word reaction line ("It's insane.", "That's it.") that lets a striking picture land. Keep it if the client says one.

## C. Layout system (our version)

12. **Split screen is the default for talking heads with proof:** proof/B-roll in the top half (y 0–960), speaker in the bottom half (y 960–1920, eyes at ≈ y 1250). Hard edge, no gradient.
13. **Rotate at least 3 layouts per reel:** split · full-frame face · full-frame B-roll · a card on ink (rounded corners 24 px, 64 px margins) with the speaker in a circle picture-in-picture (Ø 240 px, top-left, brand-colored 4 px ring). Stacked double cards for comparisons (before/after, raw/finished, you/competitor).
14. **Full-frame face on emphasis moments:** every 10–15 s, on the line that carries the point.
15. **Credit third-party footage on screen** (small @handle, Plex Mono, top-left) when it isn't the client's own.

## D. Captions and emphasis

16. **Captions sit where the layout puts them:** on the seam in split screen (centered, y ≈ 960 ± 40), at y 1360 in full-frame face, never below y 1440.
17. **3–4 words max, usually 1–3.** Karaoke word-by-word, active word in the client's accent. Small and quiet in split screen (bold, white, soft shadow, ~56–64 px), so the proof gets the attention.
18. **One big word for emphasis:** in a full-frame face shot, the key word alone, large (140–180 px), on the chest (y ≈ 1150–1250), in the accent color. Max one every ~8 s. For Buzz Media: Schibsted Grotesk ExtraBold. **We don't use his italic-serif contrast**; our contrast is size + accent color.
19. **Data reveals sit on the picture, not on a separate card:** number + unit over the B-roll it describes ("0 → 20K" counter rolling over the channel stats). A full-bleed data card is reserved for the single hero number of the reel.

## E. Structure

20. **The arc, scaled to 30 s:** hook + twist (0–5 s) → what/proof (5–15 s) → re-hook line (~12–18 s: "But here's the thing…" style, only if the client says it) → offer/takeaway (15–25 s) → CTA (last 3–5 s). For 60 s, add one more proof beat and one analogy.
21. **One analogy gets its own picture.** If the client says "it's like…", the B-roll or card shows the analogy, not the subject.
22. **Lists build visually:** each item appears as it's said (counter "1 of 3"), and a 1-line summary list at the end if the client summarizes.

## F. Endings

23. **No outro, no logo sting, no end-card hold beyond 1.0 s.** The last word ends ≤ 0.3 s before the picture does, unless the end card carries the CTA.
24. **Keyword CTA = said + shown:** the client says it ("Comment EDIT"), the end card shows it in the brand (Buzz Media: ink, red B icon, "Comment **EDIT**" with EDIT in yellow, `PLANS FROM $199/MO` in Plex Mono). End card on screen from the CTA's first word to the end (≈ 2–3 s).
25. **No CTA from the client → end on the takeaway line,** on the face with the last word large, or on the strongest B-roll frame.

## G. Sound `[EAR]`

26. **SFX are accents, not wallpaper:** a hit on the hook headline, a whoosh on layout switches *into* full-frame B-roll or cards, a soft tick per digit on counters, a pop on the big emphasis word. Never on every cut. Budget: ~1 SFX per 3–4 s, −14 to −18 dB under voice, never on a number or name being said.
27. **Music bed under everything,** low (−24 to −28 dB under voice), mid-tempo (100–130 bpm) with a clear pulse; duck under speech. Start it at frame 0 (no fade-in).
28. **Land the big moments on the beat:** the hook headline, layout switches into full-frame, and the emphasis words go on a music downbeat when the speech allows (nudge by ≤ 2 frames; never move the voice). Routine B-roll swaps follow the speech, not the music.
29. **Master at −14 LUFS, TP ≤ −1 dBTP** (his October uploads sit at −14.3).

## What we don't copy

His fonts (the condensed hook headline face and the italic serif), his red-and-yellow plate, his desk set, his "Follow for more stories like this on tech, AI, and robotics" line, his 85 s length, and his news-explainer script formula as a template for client scripts. We take the mechanics (timing, density, layout rotation, proof-first, keyword CTA) and build them in each client's brand.
